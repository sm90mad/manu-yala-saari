BIODIVERSITY OF YALA

MEET THE WILDLIFE OF YALA

Covering 978 square kilometers of monsoon forests, dry grasslands, coastal lagoons and beaches, Yala is a world-class sanctuary for Sri Lanka's most iconic species.

Apex Predator

Sri Lankan Leopard

Panthera pardus kotiya is Sri Lanka's apex predator. Yala boasts one of the highest known leopard densities on Earth.

Gentle Giants

Asian Elephant

Elephas maximus maximus. Herds roam the lush lagoons and scrublands, particularly around watering holes in late afternoons.

Shy & Elusive

Sloth Bear

Melursus ursinus inornatus. Highly active during the Palu fruit season in May-July, foraging for termites and berries.

Ancient Reptile

Mugger Crocodile

Crocodylus palustris. Commonly observed basking on sandy banks of Buthuwa lake and quiet coastal estuaries.

Wetland Inhabitant

Water Buffalo

Wallowing in freshwater marshes and saline lagoons to cool down from the equatorial tropical heat.

Herbivore Herds

Spotted Deer

Axis axis ceylonensis. Graceful herds graze across open glades, always alert and issuing sharp alarm barks when a leopard is near.

Vibrant Display

Indian Peafowl

The iconic peacock dances with iridescent tail plumage across Yala's grasslands and perches atop tall palu trees at sunrise.

215+ Species

Exotic Birdlife

From Malabar Pied Hornbills and Green Bee-eaters to White-bellied Sea Eagles, Painted Storks, and migratory wetland visitors.

Ethical Wildlife Note: All animals in Yala National Park live freely in their protected natural ecosystem. Wildlife sightings depend on natural animal movements, weather conditions, and seasonal patterns. As responsible safari operators, we respect wildlife welfare, follow park regulations, and do not guarantee sightings.
