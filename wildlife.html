ICONIC MAMMALS

THE BIG THREE OF SRI LANKA

Yala is celebrated worldwide as one of the few national parks where you can encounter Sri Lanka's renowned "Big Three" predators and mega-herbivores.

APEX CARNIVORE

Sri Lankan Leopard

Panthera pardus kotiya • Endangered Subspecies

Unlike African leopards that live alongside lions and hyenas, the Sri Lankan leopard evolved as the island's unchallenged apex predator. Because they face no competition from larger felines, they are significantly bolder, frequently seen resting atop massive granite boulders or walking along dirt trails during daylight hours.

Peak Sighting Time
06:00 – 08:30 AM & 16:30 – 18:00 PM
Habitat
Granite inselbergs, dense scrubland & Palatupana
MEGA-HERBIVORE

Asian Elephant

Elephas maximus maximus • Distinct Subspecies

The Sri Lankan elephant is the largest of the Asian elephant subspecies. In Yala, herds numbering between 5 to 20 individuals congregate around waterholes such as Buthuwa, Mahaseelawa, and Uraniya lagoons. Solitary tuskers—rare in Sri Lanka, where less than 7% of males carry tusks—are occasionally spotted roaming the forested sectors.

Peak Sighting Time
Late afternoon (15:00 – 18:00 PM)
Habitat
Lagoon edges, mud wallows & riverine forests
NOCTURNAL FORAGER

Sri Lankan Sloth Bear

Melursus ursinus inornatus • Shaggy Omnivore

With its thick shaggy black coat, sickle-shaped ivory claws, and pale snout, the sloth bear is one of Yala's most thrilling encounters. Equipped with specialized lips that can form a suction tube, they feed on termites, ants, and wild honeycombs. During the Palu tree fruiting season (May to July), sightings surge dramatically.

Prime Season
May, June, July (Palu Fruit Season)
Behavior
Termite digging & tree foraging
RICH ECOSYSTEM

REPTILES, HERBIVORES & BIRD LIFE

Beyond the big cats and elephants, Yala is a thriving biodiversity hotspot home to hundreds of fascinating species.

Wetland Predator

Mugger Crocodile

Reaching up to 4 meters in length, these prehistoric reptiles inhabit freshwater wewas (tanks) and estuaries, often seen warming on sandy banks.

Herbivore Herds

Spotted Deer

The primary prey of the leopard. Their loud alarm calls alert other animals—and our experienced safari trackers—to nearby feline movements.

Lagoon Giant

Wild Water Buffalo

Sturdy horns and immense strength, roaming in herds across coastal wetlands and cooling down submerged up to their nostrils.

Avian Majesty

Indian Peafowl

Abundant across Yala. The male's iridescent blue-green display courtship rituals are a spectacular photographic highlight.
