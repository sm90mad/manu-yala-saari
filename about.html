OUR STORY & VALUES

BORN FROM THE WILD HEART OF SRI LANKA

Manu Yala Safari Tours is an authentic local safari operator based on the Tissamaharama – Kataragama Road, dedicated to sharing the raw majesty of Yala National Park.

LOCAL HERITAGE

ROOTED IN TISSAMAHARAMA

For us, Yala is not a commercial itinerary; it is our backyard, our childhood playground, and our lifelong passion.

Located right in the gateway township of Tissamaharama on the ancient road to Kataragama, Manu Yala Safari Tours was founded with a clear vision: to provide personalized, high-standard safari experiences while preserving the wild spirit and dignity of Sri Lanka's wildlife.

Unlike large mass-tour agencies that rush guests through congested tracks, we focus on intimate, unhurried expeditions. We read the subtle signs of the wild—the frantic chatter of spotted deer announcing a leopard on the prowl, fresh pugmarks pressed into dawn mud, or the scent of damp foliage by a forest stream.

"A true safari is not about checking an animal off a checklist; it is about sharing a silent, breathless moment with a wild creature in its kingdom."
— Manu, Founder & Safari Lead
OUR CODE OF ETHICS

RESPONSIBLE WILDLIFE TOURISM

We believe that genuine wildlife enthusiasts want an authentic encounter conducted with the utmost respect for animal welfare and conservation.

No Animal Crowding

We maintain respectful distances from leopards, elephant herds, and nesting birds. We never corner, harass, or box in animals to force a photograph.

Silent Engine Philosophy

When animals are sighted, our drivers cut the engine whenever feasible to eliminate exhaust fumes and mechanical noise, creating a peaceful experience.

Strict Park Compliance

We adhere strictly to Sri Lanka Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC) guidelines, staying on sanctioned trails and honoring park opening and closing hours.

Zero Plastic Litter

We carry all trash, bottles, and food containers out of the park. We actively educate guests on preserving Yala's sensitive coastal ecosystems.
